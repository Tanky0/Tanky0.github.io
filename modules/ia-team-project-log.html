Team, Contract and Roles

Roles were allocated at the kick-off on 31 July and not rotated, since a short project gains more from continuity than breadth. A.N. emailed the signed contract to the tutor on 1 August on the group’s behalf.

Contract terms shaping the work: a fixed word budget per section, cut back by its own author if exceeded; one master document with a named owner; no project lead, decisions by agreement then majority; a tie-break route for a two-two split; and an agreed style note before drafting.

The agreed use case is a scientific literature search assistant, scoped to abstracts and open-access sources on free APIs since full text is largely paywalled. That limitation is not hidden. It is one of the challenges my own section addresses.

My Contribution

As Group Coordinator I track the schedule, though decisions belong to the group. My writing workstream is the anticipated challenges, the graphical designs and the final document review, shared with A.B.

I posted the opening proposal on 29 July so the kick-off could start from a draft. Two judgements in it have held. First, 1,000 words across four people is the binding constraint, so depth must come from precise module vocabulary rather than volume. Second, we should commit early to the Unit 3 and 4 architecture taxonomy, to Belief-Desire-Intention terminology as Rao and Georgeff (1995) set it out, and to specifying inter-agent communication through an agent communication language and a shared ontology. Saying plainly that I did not want the architecture let the work settle where the confidence was.

I then produced a requirements analysis across eleven categories, deliberately larger than the report can use, so the requirements and challenges sections could select from it with the reasoning attached.

Forum post to the requirements section owner · 3 August 2026

Five requirements carried across from clinical operations: traceability as a pass-or-fail criterion, version status as a field, reproducible search, a declared language scope, and an explicit exclusion of clinical decision support. I flagged what I had not verified, and recorded that I had reversed my own position on translation between drafts. The substantive argument was against listing capabilities we cannot build on a free tier, since naming exclusions is more defensible than claiming coverage we cannot deliver.
