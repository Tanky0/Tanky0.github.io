The Activity

Unit 2 · Run 28 October 2026 · Tutor: Dr Elizabeth Oladapo · Evidences learning outcome 2

Activity guidance: complete the exploratory data analysis tutorial on the Ames house-price data, then undertake similar analysis of the Auto MPG dataset: identify missing values, estimate skewness and kurtosis, draw a correlation heat map and scatter plots, and replace the categorical origin values with numbers.

Findings

Findings
TaskResult
Missing valuesA default load reported none. Six horsepower entries held ‘?’, so the column loaded as text. Parsed, they were left missing (392 of 398 rows); a median fill moved the horsepower to mpg correlation by only 0.005.
Skewness and kurtosisAll seven skewness estimates are positive; horsepower is the most skewed (1.09, excess kurtosis 0.70). Ames SalePrice skews 1.88, and 0.12 after a log transform.
Correlation and scatter plotsWeight, displacement and horsepower correlate with mpg at −0.78 or stronger, and with each other at 0.86 or more, a collinearity to assess before regression. For horsepower, Spearman’s rho (−0.85) exceeds Pearson’s r (−0.78), which suggests a curved relationship.
EncodingOrigin arrives coded 1, 2, 3. The labels were restored and one-hot encoded, since a model would otherwise read the codes as an order.

The main finding is that a column loaded as text can hide missing values behind a token, so parsing types and checking ranges come before modelling. The data also carry obligations: the UCI release is licensed under CC BY 4.0 and needs attributing (Quinlan, 1993), as the house data need De Cock (2011). The cars are US-market models from 1970 to 1982, so fuel-economy claims for today’s cars would be an extrapolation.
