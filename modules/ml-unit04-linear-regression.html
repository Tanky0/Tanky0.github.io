Task A (201 economies)Pearson correlation of mean population with mean GDP per person, 2001 to 2021r = −0.0667: almost no straight-line relationship.
Task B (201 economies)Linear regression of mean GDP per person on mean populationSlope −US$11.66 per million residents; R² 0.00445 on the fitted data.
Holdout check (160 / 41)Refitted on 160 economies and predicted the other 41Test R² −0.038; mean absolute error US$14,531 against US$14,860 for the training mean: no useful gain.

Population alone says almost nothing about GDP per person: the line is nearly flat, and on held-out countries it predicts no better than the average. The main lesson was in the preparation. The supplied GDP file held total GDP, not GDP per person, and a default load would not have shown it. Keeping only complete records may favour countries with better reporting, and World Bank data must be credited under its CC BY 4.0 licence (World Bank, 2026b).
