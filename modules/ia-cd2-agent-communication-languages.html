Collaborative Discussion 2: Agent Communication Languages

Whether an agent communication language such as KQML earns its cost against ordinary method invocation, argued across an initial post, two peer responses and a summary.

The Activity

Units 5–7 · Due end of Unit 7 · Evidences learning outcomes 1 and 4

Discussion question: What are the potential advantages and disadvantages of the use of agent communication languages such as KQML? How do they compare with method invocation in Python or Java?

Structure: Initial post of at least 200 words in Unit 5, labelled Initial Post. Two peer responses in Unit 6 of 200–300 words each, labelled Peer Response, engaging with the positions classmates take on where an agent communication language earns its cost. A 300-word Summary Post in Unit 7 drawing on the initial post, the peer feedback and the content of all three units. UoEO Harvard referencing throughout.

In progress: Both peer responses are posted, one to K.D. and one to L.K., each pressing the same point from a different direction: that KQML’s openness was never the difficulty, since Finin et al. (1994, p. 460) allow agents to add performatives by agreement, and what was never enforced was that two systems read them the same way. The summary post follows in Unit 7, and all four pieces will then be added together on their own page, as for Collaborative Discussion 1.
