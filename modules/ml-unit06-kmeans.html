Activity guidance: run the supplied k-means demo, then cluster at least one of three datasets: iris and wine at k = 3, compared with their labels (Tasks A and B), and weatherAUS for k = 2 to 6, shown on two-dimensional scatter plots (Task C).

Findings

Findings
Step (n)What I didResult
Demo (850 customers)Made five corrections, then refitted k = 3 on the scaled dataThe clusters now come from scaled data with a fixed seed; the 150 missing default values are counted rather than hidden.
Task A: iris (150 flowers)Tried k = 2 to 8, then compared k = 3 with the species134 of 150 matched (89.3%), adjusted Rand index 0.730 (Hubert and Arabie, 1985, cited in Arabie and Hubert, 1992). Z-scores lowered this to 83.3% and range scaling to 88.7%; the silhouette score was highest at k = 2.
Task B: wine (178 wines)Compared k = 3 with the three cultivars, on raw, z-scored and range-scaled measurementsZ-scores: 172 of 178 matched (96.6%), index 0.897. Range: 94.9%. Raw: 70.2%, index 0.371, although its silhouette score was higher.
Task C: weather (120,381 days)Removed the rain columns and incomplete rows, standardised, then tried k = 2 to 6Silhouette scores of 0.16 to 0.24 at every k; k = 2 split warmer, less humid days from cooler, more humid ones. Filling gaps with medians instead gave almost the same k = 3 clusters (index 0.968).

A tidy set of clusters is not proof that the groups exist. The silhouette score (Rousseeuw, 1987) disagreed with the labels twice: it preferred two iris groups although three species are present, and raw wine scored higher than z-scored wine while matching far worse. Scaling decided the wine result, yet z-scores, although Géron (2025) says scaling generally helps, lowered the iris match. Dividing by the range, which Milligan and Cooper (1988, cited in Arabie and Hubert, 1992) found better, came close to the best on both. Without location or date, the weather clusters cannot be interpreted by station or season. I would record scaling and missing-data choices for whoever acts on the clusters.
