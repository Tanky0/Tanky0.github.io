Module Overview

Numerical Analysis develops the mathematical and statistical foundations that underpin Data Science and Artificial Intelligence, taught and assessed through applied work in R. The module moves from descriptive statistics and data visualisation, through hypothesis testing and non-parametric alternatives, to correlation, linear and logistic regression, and finally Bayesian reasoning. Each unit adds a technique alongside a harder question about what the evidence can legitimately be asked to show.

Coming into the module with no prior programming experience, the practical work in R (readr, dplyr, haven, ggplot2) represented the steepest learning curve of the programme to date. The module's central contribution to my professional practice has been the shift from treating statistical output as a verdict to treating it as an argument that must be interrogated: which method was used, whether it suited the data, and what the result can actually support.

Module Aims and Learning Outcomes

In this module, we shall acquire:

  • Develop a systematic understanding of foundational mathematical principles and methods, as well as core and specialised concepts underpinning computing logic.
  • Understand the foundation for the development and application of programming and data-driven techniques, from both a theoretical and practical viewpoint.
  • Facilitate the ability to interpret the results generated when using these data science and AI tools.
  • Gain an understanding of the real-world applications of these computational tools, and contemporary issues related to these computational techniques.
  • The opportunity to take a reflective and independent approach to the learning process.

On completion of this module, you will be able to:

  1. LO1: Demonstrate systematic understanding of the key mathematical and statistical concepts and techniques which underpin mechanisms in Data Science and AI.
  2. LO2: Apply mathematical and statistical methods in these fields to help in the decision-making process.
  3. LO3: Critically evaluate the use of statistical analysis and the numeric interpretation of results as aids in the decision-making process.
  4. LO4: Critically appraise and present results of a statistical analysis to a diverse audience.

End of Module Assessment 1: Statistical Analysis Presentation

Brief Description: A 20-minute recorded presentation with transcript analysing the Pima Indian Diabetes Database (768 women, nine variables). The brief required data exploration and descriptive statistics with appropriate visualisations and outlier discussion; diabetes rates across age, BMI and pregnancy categories; and inferential work covering hypothesis testing, a correlation matrix, chi-square association tests, ANOVA across age groups, a multiple linear regression predicting glucose, a logistic regression predicting diabetes outcome evaluated with Hosmer-Lemeshow and classification metrics, and likelihood ratio tests for a BMI×Age interaction. Conclusions and recommendations were required throughout. All analysis and visualisation was performed in R using RStudio.

Approach taken: Zero values in glucose, blood pressure, BMI, skin thickness and insulin were treated as missing rather than valid, reducing the regression sample from 768 to 392 and creating a missingness problem reported openly as a limitation. Where normality could not be assumed, non-parametric tests were run alongside parametric ones (Welch's t-test with Wilcoxon rank-sum; ANOVA with Kruskal-Wallis) rather than relying on a single result. Diagnostic plots were worked through in detail: the scale-location plot showed error variance rising with predicted glucose, meaning confidence intervals become least reliable exactly where diabetic cases concentrate. The logistic model reached 77% accuracy and 87.9% specificity but only 56.8% sensitivity at the 0.50 threshold — 114 missed diagnoses — which drove the recommendation to lower the threshold to 0.30–0.35 and to adopt multiple imputation, cross-validation, and Bayesian logistic regression before any clinical use.
