The CNN Explainer network tested on its own examples, synthetic images and my photos: no confidence threshold separated what it knows from what it does not.

The Activity

Unit 10 · Run 12 November 2026 · Tutor: Dr Elizabeth Oladapo · Evidences learning outcomes 1 and 2

Activity guidance: read about convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and watch the video tutorial, look at the image recognition at different layers (ReLU, intermediate), and upload my own similar and dissimilar images to test how the CNN detects objects like those it was trained on. CNN Explainer runs Tiny VGG, trained on ten Tiny ImageNet classes to 70.8% top-1 validation accuracy (Wang et al., 2021). I loaded its weights in Colab so that every score for this network comes from one run.

PreparationCentre crop, padding and a browser-like resize for each imageThe top class changed for every photo and for none of the synthetic images. Replacing the resizing routine in Colab changed the blanket cat from espresso to pizza; CNN Explainer also said pizza.
ThresholdCounted what a minimum score would acceptAny threshold high enough to reject the wrench would also reject six of the ten built-in examples. At 0.90, seven are kept while two, one or none of the five outside images pass, depending on the preparation.

I expected confusion with things of a similar type, shape or colour: red panda for my two ginger cats and koala for my grey one. The teacup was labelled espresso, as I expected, but fur colour led to neither red panda nor koala: all three cats became espresso. With no “unknown” class, the highest softmax score always wins (Wang et al., 2021), and no threshold separated familiar from unfamiliar images here; detecting unfamiliar inputs is a separate task (Yang et al., 2024). Before sign-off, I would ask how often an abstention rule accepts outside images and rejects valid ones, with one fixed input pipeline.
